1. Today's Take

Yesterday was a flat-out sprint in the online market. We saw $6,628,926.35 shift hands across 26 distinct six-figure deals, with Bring a Trailer driving almost the entire volume ($6.36M over 25 sales) and Cars & Bids chipping in a single GT3 sale. Buyers are not sitting on their hands—they are aggressively pursuing top-tier specs and paying up when the right car drops.

What caught my eye wasn't just the sheer transaction volume, but where the money is landing. Clean, highly optioned modern GT track weapons and last-of-the-breed naturally aspirated engines continue to act as safe havens for capital. It feels like any hint of a summer lull is officially behind us as we move deeper into August.

4. What to Watch

Modern JDM Collectibles Are Stepping Into Supercar Territories

The sale of the 2016 Nissan GT-R Nismo at $275,500 is a massive wake-up call for anyone who still thinks of Godzillas as bargain supercars. Low-mileage Nismo-spec GT-Rs are stepping squarely into supercar-royalty valuation territory. If you own a clean, unmodified R35 Nismo, hold onto your keys because the runway for these cars is long and collector interest is only growing as the generation ages.

Naturally Aspirated Masterpieces Pulling Big Premiums

Yesterday we watched a 2015 Ferrari 458 Italia hit $508,500 and a 2019 Ferrari 812 Superfast pull $573,500. The 458 sale is particularly wild—half a million for a non-Speciale, standard-production 458 is serious money. But it represents the last of the naturally aspirated, Pininfarina-designed V8 Ferraris, and collectors are clearly willing to pay a premium for that direct acoustic feedback. Meanwhile, the 812 Superfast continues to prove that front-engine V12 grand tourers remain the ultimate blue-chip choice for collectors who want raw, unadulterated Italian engineering.

5. High-End Price Breakdown & Valuation Spread (7-Day Rolling Context)

The distribution over the past week shows a highly predictable concentration. The entry-level luxury bracket ($100k - $250k) is the undisputed powerhouse of transaction volume, commanding 94 out of 141 total sales (roughly 66.7%).

7. What's Coming Up This Week

With Monterey Car Week just around the corner, the physical auction houses are starting to drop their catalog highlights. We are already seeing incredible previews from RM Sotheby's, Gooding & Company, and Broad Arrow, including some seven-figure vintage Ferraris and pre-merger AMGs. It is the busiest time of year for collector car logistics, and the smart money is already planning which physical auctions to hit versus which digital platforms to monitor for distracted bidders.

On the digital front, keep your eyes glued to BaT and duPont REGISTRY Live as several online sellers try to sneak high-end reserves under the radar while the community's attention is divided by the physical events. It is a prime opportunity to score a deal on a modern supercar if the major collectors are busy sipping champagne on the peninsula. Keep your alerts active and your capital ready.
