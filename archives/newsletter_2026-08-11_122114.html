🎙️ 1. Today's Take

The high-end online market continues to show incredible resilience, proving that buyers aren't afraid to write serious checks when the right car comes along. This past week saw 16 six-figure transactions close, bringing in a total volume of over $7.75 million. The story here isn't just the sheer dollar amount—it’s the type of metal finding new homes. Collectors are heavily prioritizing analog driver's cars. Look no further than the Carrera GT and the Lexus LFA leading the pack; buyers are happily paying a massive premium for visceral driving experiences rather than chasing the absolute newest hypercars.

While Bring a Trailer continues to haul in the biggest individual results, duPont REGISTRY Live is quietly carving out a strong position in the ultra-competitive $250k to $500k bracket. We are seeing a healthy spread across modern GT cars, track weapons, and blue-chip classics. If you’ve been waiting on the sidelines for a massive market correction, you might be waiting a while. The right specs are holding value, and serious collectors are still very much active.

👁️ 3. What to Watch

Analog Engagement vs. Hybrid Technology

We are seeing a massive valuation spread between analog legends and ultra-modern hybrid supercars. Take a look at the 2005 Porsche Carrera GT pulling in $1,350,000, while the heavily digital, 986-horsepower 2021 Ferrari SF90 Stradale finished at $510,000. Despite being significantly slower on a track, the Carrera GT is worth nearly triple the price. The takeaway here is simple: collectors prioritize raw driver engagement and historical significance over pure, digital-heavy performance numbers.

The Modern Porsche GT Premium

Another key trend to watch is the secondary market for limited-run Porsche specials. The 2023 Porsche 911 S/T changed hands for $440,000. These lightweight, manual-gearbox models are commanding massive premiums over their original MSRPs. As manufacturers trend toward hybrid power units, watch for these last-of-the-breed naturally aspirated GT Porsches to stay highly insulated from depreciation.
