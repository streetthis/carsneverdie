🎙️ 1. Today's Take

We are seeing some fascinating dynamics play out in the six-and-seven-figure space right now. The total volume for $100k+ collector cars hit $17.47 million over 20 logged transactions this past week, pointing to a highly targeted buyer base. What stands out isn't a massive rush of retail buyers, but rather a sharp, surgical focus by serious collectors on blue-chip, irreplaceable assets. The air gets thin north of $2 million, but when cars like a LaFerrari or an F40 come to market with clean histories, the money is right there waiting.

At the same time, online bidding platforms continue to prove they can reliably anchor the mid-six-figure market. We watched a healthy mix of analog legends and modern track specials change hands without a hitch, demonstrating that the appetite for high-end driver's cars remains rock solid. Buyers are simply refusing to pay premiums for mediocre or over-speculated assets, but they will fight to the end for the right spec and documented provenance.

2015 Ferrari LaFerrari

👁️ 3. What to Watch

First, pay close attention to the analog supercar market, specifically vehicles like the 2005 Porsche Carrera GT (sold at $1.35M) and the 1993 Bugatti EB110 GT (sold at $2.1M). There is an obvious flight to analog engagement. As modern cars become increasingly insulated and digital, these raw, manual-transmission, high-skill collector cars are carving out a permanent, highly insulated class of their own. They aren't just cars; they are historical artifacts of an era that will never return.

Second, the 2012 Lexus LFA pulling $820k on BaT is another indicator of changing generational tastes. Ten years ago, the LFA was misunderstood; today, its Yamahah-tuned V10 is legendary, and younger Gen-X and Millennial collectors with serious capital are prioritizing it over traditional European classics. If you have been waiting for LFA prices to dip back below the half-million mark, it's time to face reality: those days are long gone.

🔥 6. What's Coming Up This Week

As we march closer to the big flagship events like Monterey Car Week, the major physical houses—RM Sotheby's, Gooding & Co, and Bonhams—are dropping previews of some mind-boggling inventory. Keep your eyes peeled for the "Big Five" Ferraris, historically significant Talbot-Lago Teardrops, and rumor has it a road-converted McLaren F1 GTR might make an appearance. These high-end catalog lots will set the baseline for valuation standards heading into the fall.

At the same time, don't ignore the live daily digital drops on BaT and duPont REGISTRY Live. The digital platforms are listing some incredible, low-mileage modern classics to capture the overflow attention from the physical auctions. My advice? If you are a buyer, do your homework now, verify the PPIs, and don't let auction-room hype make you overpay.
