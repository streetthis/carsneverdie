WHY I DID NOT SLEEP LAST NIGHT

Yesterday was a massive reality check for anyone claiming the high-end collector market is losing steam. We saw exactly $5,677,213 shift hands across 21 six-figure cars, proving that clean, highly optioned cars are still pulling crazy numbers as the post-Monterey dry powder finds its home.

What really caught my eye was the Porsche domination. Between a legendary air-cooled RS, an ultra-rare water-cooled GT2 Clubsport, and a modern manual GT3 Touring, the Zuffenhausen factory captured three of yesterday's top six spots.

But it wasn't just Porsche. The analog V12 market is in absolute overdrive. When a gated-manual Lamborghini Murcielago and a high-spec front-engine Ferrari V12 are transacting back-to-back, you know the smart money is hedging hard against an increasingly hybrid future.

VEHICLE SPOTLIGHT

This is an absolute monster of a result. While some modern British supercars suffer from the typical depreciation curve, the 765LT has proven to be the exception to the rule. This particular example in MSO Curaçao Blue crossed the block on duPont REGISTRY Live for a staggering $635,250.

With limited production numbers and a reputation as one of the most violent, unfiltered track weapons of the modern era, the LT suffix still commands a serious premium. Buyers are waking up to the fact that McLaren will likely never make something quite this raw again.

WHAT TO WATCH

We are keeping a very close eye on two major market-moving battles closing today and tomorrow:

1. High-Tier Analog Track Weapons

Following the massive $807,500 result for a 2004 Ferrari 360 Challenge Stradale earlier this week, all eyes are on Bring a Trailer today for late-model analog track specials. A 2011 Porsche 911 GT3 RS (997.2) is heading into its final hours, and we want to see if it can push past the $280,000 barrier. If the bidding momentum holds, it will prove that the premium for hydraulic steering and mechanical throttle response is still climbing.

2. Naturally Aspirated V12 GTs

With yesterday's Ferrari 599 GTB Fiorano bringing $247,800, we are watching several front-engine V12s closing today. Keep a close eye on the bidding action for modern, naturally aspirated V12 GTs. The reserves on these are notoriously tight, and a single missed bid could mean the difference between a high-water mark and a frustrating RNM. Check if the buyers who missed out on V12s in Monterey are stepping up to pay the premium online.

WHAT'S COMING UP THIS WEEK

The physical dust has settled in Monterey, and transporters are heading back across the country. But the digital marketplace is just getting started. This weekend, we are expecting a massive surge of catalog drops as unsuccessful bidders from California look to deploy their dry power. Keep your eyes on Bring a Trailer, duPont REGISTRY Live, and Cars & Bids for some incredible late-summer listings.

My advice for this weekend? Don't panic buy, but don't sleep on high-quality manual GT cars. The gap between average examples and investment-grade cars is widening fast, and the smart money is paying up for the best-of-the-best.
