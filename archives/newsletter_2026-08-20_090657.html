1. Today's Take

If you were watching the boards yesterday, you saw $6.62M shift hands across 26 six-figure cars. Almost all of that action was concentrated on Bring a Trailer, which grabbed a massive 96% market share of yesterday's volume. It is post-Monterey hangover week, but instead of taking a breather, buyers who walked away empty-handed in California are immediately moving their capital back online.

With 141 six-figure sales crossing the digital block over the past seven days, the market is absorbing inventory at a highly stable rate. Daily volume has settled back into a comfortable $6M to $7.5M run rate after the massive spike on August 15. The demand is there, but buyers are remaining highly selective about options, history, and paint.

2. Vehicle Spotlight of the Day

While the GT2 RS grabbed the top spot by pure dollar value yesterday, the real eyebrow-raiser was this clean 2019 Ferrari 812 Superfast. Standard, non-limited 812s typically trade in the high-$300k to low-$400k range. This particular example blew past those expectations, hammering for an incredible $573,500.

  • Vehicle: 2019 Ferrari 812 Superfast
  • Hammer Price: $573,500 USD
  • Auction House: Bring a Trailer
  • The Takeaway: This result shows that collectors are placing an increasingly high premium on original-owner, low-mileage V12 front-engine grand tourers. With Ferrari pushing harder into hybrid assistance across its newer lineups, the pure, naturally aspirated 6.5-liter V12 in the 812 Superfast is rapidly graduating into a modern classic. Buyers are paying up for high-option builds, original paint, and flawless documentation.

4. What to Watch (Auctions Closing Today & Tomorrow)

With Monterey wrapped up, the focus returns to the digital boards where a few key trends are playing out over the next 48 hours:

Gated Manuals and Analog V12s

Following yesterday's $247,500 sale of the 2000 Ferrari 550 Maranello, watch active analog gated 6-speed V12 listings closing today and tomorrow. There is a clear flight to safety in analog cars with three pedals. Keep a close eye on bidding momentum in the final hours to see if buyers are willing to push past standard reserves for well-sorted driver-mileage examples.

997-Generation Porsche GT3 Momentum

Yesterday's 2007 Porsche 911 GT3 hammered for $260,750, showcasing the strong premium remaining for clean 997-era Rennsport and GT models. If you are watching active listings today or tomorrow, pay close attention to over-rev reports and paint meter readings. Clean-sheet cars are commanding big premiums, while heavily modified or tracked examples are struggling to meet reserve.

7. What's Coming Up This Week

Now that the dust has settled on the physical Monterey auctions, the next seven days are all about digital consolidation. We are seeing a steady stream of fresh, late-summer catalog drops on BaT, duPont REGISTRY Live, and Cars & Bids. Many sellers who were waiting to see how the physical market behaved in California are now listing their cars, making this an incredibly active window for online buyers.

For the serious collector, this is the time to look for value. The massive hype of Car Week is behind us, and we are entering a brief seasonal window where sellers are motivated to get deals done before the autumn cool-down. Keep your alerts active, watch the comments sections for transparent sellers, and be ready to pull the trigger if a well-documented driver comes up with a reasonable reserve.
