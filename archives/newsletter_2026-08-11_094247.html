🎙️ 1. Today's Take

The high-end collector car market continues to prove that even during the typical late-summer lull, quality and rarity command serious capital. Over the past 7 days, we tracked $17.46 million moving across 20 six-figure transactions. What we are seeing is a highly concentrated market where buyers are skipping over average, high-mileage exotics to fight over pristine, collector-grade assets. The classic division between traditional physical auctions and digital volume engines remains clear, but both sides of the aisle are finding buyers when the spec is right.

What really stands out is the split in collector appetite. Traditional modern-day exotics like the SF90 Stradale are taking standard depreciation hits, while analog legends from the late 20th century are pushing deep into seven-figure territory. The appetite for raw, engaging driving experiences is highly insulated, and serious collectors are keeping their checkbooks open for no-excuses cars.

👁️ 3. What to Watch

The Great Analog Hypercar Flight

If you want to understand where the smart money is hiding, look closely at the split between the 1993 Bugatti EB110 GT hitting $2.1M and the 2021 Ferrari SF90 Stradale selling for $510k. Modern hybrid exotics are dealing with steep luxury depreciation curves, while late 20th-century analog machines are experiencing a massive collector renaissance. Raw, tactile experiences with manual gearboxes or early carbon chassis are the priority for serious long-term holds.

The Sweet Spot ($250k - $500k)

Modern-classic track weapons and limited-run manual cars are acting as excellent stores of value. The 2023 Porsche 911 S/T command of $440k and the GT2 RS at $385k show that Porsche's GT division still rules this bracket. If you are shopping in this segment, look for unique paint-to-sample specs and high-option builds, as buyers are paying a massive premium to skip the dealer waitlists.

📈 4. High-End Price Breakdown & Valuation Spread

The market distribution this week highlights where the bulk of transaction volume occurs versus where the actual cash flows:
* The $250k–$500k tier was the most active sandbox with 8 completed transactions (40% of units), representing the sweet spot for modern classics like the Porsche GT2 RS, Ford GT, and Ferrari 488 Pista.
* The $2M+ hypercar tier was where the massive capital went, representing only 3 units but capturing 49.2% of the week's total cash flow ($8.60 million).

  • $100k-$250k (3 units, $0.485M total): Divided equally between Barrett-Jackson (Corvette L88), Bring a Trailer (Jaguar E-Type), and PCARMARKET (911 Speedster).
  • $250k-$500k (8 units, $3.13M total): Dominated overwhelmingly by Bring a Trailer (6 of 8 units), with Broad Arrow (Shelby GT350) and PCARMARKET (911 S/T) taking one apiece.
  • $500k-$750k (2 units, $1.13M total): Shared between RM Sotheby's (911 Carrera RS 2.7) and BaT (Ferrari SF90).
  • $750k-$1M (3 units, $2.75M total): Broad Arrow captured one Cobra 289 ($950k) while BaT took the remaining two (McLaren Senna at $980k and Lexus LFA at $820k).
  • $1M-$2M (1 unit, $1.35M total): Solely occupied by Bring a Trailer with the 2005 Porsche Carrera GT sale.
  • $2M+ (3 units, $8.60M total): Unconditionally controlled by RM Sotheby's, capturing 100% market share in this ultra-high-end tier (Bugatti EB110 GT, Ferrari F40, and Ferrari LaFerrari).

🔥 6. What's Coming Up This Week

The major physical auction houses are finalising their catalogs, and the upcoming Monterey Car Week previews are starting to take over the collector conversation. Expect to see historic numbers on the horizon from RM Sotheby's, Gooding & Co, and Bonhams featuring holy grails like the "Big Five" Ferraris, Talbot-Lago Teardrops, and legendary McLaren F1 GTR chassis.

While the physical catalog auctions will claim the headliner space, don’t ignore the daily digital drops. Platforms like BaT and duPont REGISTRY Live are consistently dropping highly spec’d modern performance cars that bypass the traditional auction schedule. For serious collectors, keeping cash liquid is key right now—opportunities to pick up slightly depreciated modern exotics are popping up daily, and patience is paying off.
