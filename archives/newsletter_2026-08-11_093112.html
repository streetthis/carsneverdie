🎙️ 1. Today's Take

We saw some serious money moving through the market this week, with over $44.5M changing hands across 167 high-end lots selling north of the $100k mark. If anyone was worried that high-end collector sentiment was cooling off, this week’s numbers should put those fears to rest. Porsche absolutely ruled the field, securing 44% of the total weekly market volume and proving that both modern-day hypercars and well-executed restomods are still the safest bets for parking serious capital.

The mid-week peak hit a massive $11.8M on August 6th, largely driven by ultra-exclusive modern performance machinery finding new garages. While the bulk of the market stays pinned to the $100k-$250k tier, buyers are not hesitating to fight for clean, low-mileage examples of the "Holy Trinity" or historically significant race cars. Quality and provenance are still driving the biggest premiums, and the top tier of the market is holding remarkably firm.

👁️ 3. What to Watch

Custom Restomods vs. Factory Originals

Take a look at the 1989 Porsche 911 Carrera Classic Turbo by Singer fetching a massive $1.67M this week, compared to a highly collectible, numbers-matching factory original like the 1994 Porsche 911 Turbo 3.6 at $777k. While purists love a factory-correct car, today's high-end buyers are paying twice as much for elite, bespoke restomod commissions that blend retro looks with modern performance. If you are holding an allocation for a top-tier build, the market is firmly in your favor.

The Premium on Analog Mechanical Engagement

Three-pedal manual collector cars are commanding massive premiums right now. We watched the 2006 Ford GT Heritage Edition clear the $2.1M mark, and the highly anticipated, manual-only 2024 Porsche 911 S/T fetch $578k. Even as modern hypercars get quicker and more complex, collectors are paying a massive premium for tactile, analog experiences. Focus on limited-run manual driver's cars; they are where the smart money is moving.
