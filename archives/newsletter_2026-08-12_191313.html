🎙️ 1. Today's Take

Yesterday saw a respectable $4,311,302 in high-end ($100k+) transaction volume, representing a solid midweek baseline. Bring a Trailer continued to act as the heavy lifter, absorbing 20 of the day's 21 six-figure sales. While the wild $11.27M peak from August 6th (driven by that seven-figure 918 Spyder) is now in the rearview mirror, yesterday's volume proves that high-end collectors are still actively hunting for quality front-engine V12s and air-cooled metal.

Buyer sentiment is selective but firm. We aren't seeing blind bidding frenzies on average cars, but highly optioned, low-mileage flagships and well-executed re-creations are easily drawing strong six-figure numbers. It is a market that rewards patience and exceptional presentation rather than pure speculative hype.

👁️ 3. What to Watch

  • Air-Cooled 993 Manuals Remain Bulletproof: Look at the results of the 1998 Porsche 911 Carrera S Coupe 6-Speed ($332,500) and the 1998 Porsche 911 Carrera 4S Coupe ($200,000). While the Carrera S commanded a premium due to its rear-wheel-drive configuration and lower production numbers, both of these widebody 993s demonstrate that collectors are willing to pay significant six-figure sums for the last of the air-cooled era. If you are looking to enter this space, focus on pristine paint, documented service history, and a gated manual gearbox; these configurations are effectively decoupled from broader economic corrections.
  • The Modern V12 Premium vs. Mid-Engine V8s: The 2019 Ferrari 812 Superfast closing at $553,500 and the 2000 Ferrari 550 Maranello at $237,500 highlight the sustained demand for front-engine V12s. Compare that with the mid-engine 2011 Ferrari 458 Italia which sold for $297,500. While the 458 is widely praised as the pinnacle of naturally aspirated V8 Ferraris, it is clear that front-engine V12 road cars—whether classic manual cruisers like the 550 or modern hyper-GTs like the 812—are the primary long-term wealth preservers in the modern Ferrari catalog.

📈 4. High-End Price Breakdown & Valuation Spread (7-Day Rolling Context)

Over the past 7 days, we tracked 174 six-figure transactions. The vast majority of the volume sits in the entry-level six-figure tier, with 128 sales in the $100k - $250k range. This is the bread-and-butter of the online space, heavily populated by late-model 911s, daily-driver supercars, and classic restomods. As we move up the ladder, the air thins out: 30 sales landed in the $250k - $500k tier, followed by 7 sales in the $500k - $750k band, and 6 sales in the $750k - $1M bracket. Seven-figure territory was highly exclusive, with just 2 sales in the $1M - $2M range and 1 crowning sale over $2M (the $4.01M Porsche 918 Spyder on duPont REGISTRY Live).

🔥 6. What's Coming Up This Week

As we look ahead, the collector car universe is bracing for the crown jewel of the auction calendar: Monterey Car Week. The catalog previews from RM Sotheby's, Gooding & Company, and Broad Arrow are dropping, teasing some of the finest vintage Ferraris, rare pre-war classics, and low-mileage homologation specials to ever hit the block. While these flagship live auctions will command seven- and eight-figure headlines in the coming weeks, the online sector isn't slowing down. Expect a flurry of daily high-end digital drops on Bring a Trailer and duPont REGISTRY Live as sellers look to capture the attention of high-net-worth buyers who are already priming their bank accounts for Monterey.

For serious collectors, the next 7 days are about discipline. Keep an eye on high-reserve online lots that fail to meet their reserves. Often, motivated sellers who miss their mark online will be highly receptive to post-auction deals right before they head to the physical auctions in California. Watch the bidding patterns on high-end Porsches and Ferraris closely; they will serve as the ultimate barometer for the physical market's temperature in Monterey.
