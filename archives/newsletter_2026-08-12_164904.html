🎙️ 1. Today's Take

The high-end online collector market ($100k+) is firing on all cylinders, logging an impressive 168 six-figure sales for a massive $44.19M in total volume over the past 7 days. While Bring a Trailer remains the absolute volume king, the big story this week is the willingness of buyers to drop multi-million dollar checks entirely online. The fact that a Porsche 918 Spyder crossed the $4 million mark via a digital catalog shows that the top end of the market has fully embraced the online format without hesitation.

Porsche continues to be the safest place to park capital, accounting for nearly half of the entire week’s volume. However, the market is separating into two distinct lanes: a highly liquid commodity space for standard modern GT cars in the $100k to $250k bracket, and a hyper-selective tier where historical significance, unique specs, and pristine preservation drive bidding wars past the million-dollar line.

👁️ 3. What to Watch

Road-Registered Motorsport Legends

We're seeing a shift in what ultra-high-net-worth collectors want to store in their garages. Take a look at the 1988 Porsche Kremer 962 Group C Endurance Race Car that sold for over $1.08M. It’s not just a historic race car with ex-Andretti and Le Mans provenance; it’s road-registered. The appetite for raw, historically significant motorsport vehicles that can be driven to events or utilized in historic racing series is eclipsing the interest in generic modern hypercars. Real provenance is the ultimate hedge.

The Return to Analog V12s

The solid results for the 1997 Lamborghini Diablo VT Roadster ($835,500) and the 1971 Ferrari 365 GTB/4 Daytona Berlinetta ($872,500) tell a clear story. Buyers are paying top dollar for classic twelve-cylinder experiences with gated gearboxes or direct mechanical linkages. As manufacturers phase out pure combustion engines, these analog cars are being tucked away as permanent historic relics. If you own an analog V12, do not sell cheap—this market segment is incredibly sticky.
