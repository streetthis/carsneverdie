*4. What to Watch*

*Modern Front-Engine V12 Resiliency*
The sale of a 2019 Ferrari 812 Superfast (https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2019-ferrari-812-superfast-31/) yesterday for $573,500 is a major data point. This represents a highly resilient value curve for modern front-engine V12 grand tourers. As Ferrari transitions its lineup toward hybridization and turbocharged powerplants, collectors are actively parking cash in late-model, naturally aspirated twelve-cylinder powerhouses. High-option list prices are holding firm, and clean examples are commanding close to their original MSRPs.

*The Late-Model Analog Premium*
We also witnessed a 2015 Ferrari 458 Italia (https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2015-ferrari-458-italia-18/) change hands for a staggering $508,500. For a non-Speciale, this is a massive premium. It signals that low-mileage, exceptionally preserved paint-to-sample or highly optioned specs of the last naturally aspirated mid-engine V8 are finding an elite tier of buyers who are willing to pay near-supercar money to avoid the complexity of modern turbocharged replacements.
